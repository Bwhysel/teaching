# FIRST KEYS: INSTRUCTOR ANSWER KEY (v2)

*Generated from the game's own scoring function, so this key and the game cannot disagree. Every example below was run through the game and earns 10 points.*

## Scoring
- **10 points:** meets the client's goal, uses the loan the lesson wants (where there is one), and the price is within 10% of the highest price that fits.
- **5 points:** a bank would approve it (total debt at most 43% of income, cash after closing at least 3 months of payments), but it misses the client's goal, uses a weaker loan, or is more than 10% below the ceiling.
- **0 points:** a bank would not approve it.
- **Total:** 80 points (8 x 10). One submit per client; "Play Again" restarts the game.

**Minimum down payments (enforced by the slider):** conventional 5%, FHA 3.5%, VA 0%. These are the figures the game's own Loan Types tooltip states. They are simplifications, so confirm them before you teach them as current rules.

**Goals.** "Max" clients want the most expensive home a bank will approve (total debt up to 43%; above 36% is "expensive"). "Comfort" clients (Cases 3 and 6) want housing at most 28% and total debt at most 36% of income.

## Mango in this game
- **Role:** a bank rep. She is reliable when she explains the math and unreliable when she gives advice.
- **Tells:** 😼 means the advice favors the bank, and the trick is named in the tooltip (Conflict of interest, Appeal to emotion, Wishful thinking, Cynicism). 😺 means she is explaining the math (definitions and ratios), and that can be trusted.
- **In the mood:** in Cases 4 and 8 she purrs and grudgingly explains the Loan Amount tooltip.
- **Front page:** says she helps if she is in the mood, and that 😼 is the tell.

## Facts to verify before students see Case 7
Case 7 models NYC HomeFirst. These details came from search results and secondary sources, not the official pages (the research container could not reach nyc.gov). Confirm them with NYC HPD or a HUD-approved counseling agency:
- 0% forgivable loan, up to the lesser of 20% of the purchase price or $100,000 ([NYC HPD announcement](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/news/055-21/homefirst-down-payment-assistance-program-now-offering-first-time-buyers-up-100-000-support))
- Buyer must contribute at least 3% of the price from their own funds, no more than half of it gifted ([ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/finance/new-york-first-time-homebuyer-programs.html))
- Income at most 120% of area median income; first-time buyer (no home owned in the past 3 years); HPD-approved homebuyer education certificate (same source)
- Forgiven after 10 years (loans of $40,000 or less) or 15 years (larger), if the home is the primary residence (same source)
- **Simplification in the game:** the game uses the maximum amount HomeFirst allows. Real awards depend on the buyer's financial need and underwriting.
- The game's mortgage rate (6.75%) is an assumption, not a quoted rate.
